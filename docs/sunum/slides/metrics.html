Why AUC? Reading the metrics

AUC (area under the ROC curve)

Pick one topic with a confidential document and one without. AUC is the probability that the decoder scores the first one higher.

• Needs no threshold, so it does not depend on how the decoder is tuned.
• Not fooled by class imbalance, unlike accuracy.
• One number a reader can compare across models and modes.

How to read it

0.5

1.0

0.5 = coin flip, no leakage.
1.0 = perfect detection.
Below 0.5 can appear by noise with leave-one-topic-out, so we judge by the permutation p-value too.

TPR @ 1% FPR

Share of secrets caught when only 1% false alarms are allowed: a cautious attacker.

Messages needed

How many probes until topic-level AUC reaches 0.9.

Leaked bits

Information per message, in bits (1 bit = a fair coin flip resolved).

BLG558E · Security & Privacy · Final Project

Why AUC rather than accuracy: a decoder outputs a score, and accuracy depends on where we cut it. AUC sums over all cuts and equals the probability that a random positive topic outranks a random negative topic. It is also stable when the classes are unbalanced. We add TPR at 1% false-positive rate because a real attacker usually cannot afford many false alarms, messages-needed because leakage per message matters in practice, and leaked bits as an information-theoretic summary. With leave-one-topic-out, noisy data can push AUC below 0.5; that is why the permutation p-value and the advantage over the shuffled baseline are our decision criteria, not the raw AUC.
