Related work, all checked against the arXiv or publisher pages. (1) Weiss, Ayzenshteyn, Amit, Mirsky, "What Was Your Prompt? A Remote Keylogging Attack on AI Assistants", USENIX Security 2024, arXiv 2403.09751: a token-length side channel in encrypted streaming responses. (2) Carlini and Nasr, "Remote Timing Attacks on Efficient Language Model Inference", arXiv 2410.17175: timing differences from efficient inference reveal conversation topics. (3) Microsoft Research, "Whisper Leak: a side-channel attack on Large Language Models", arXiv 2511.03675: packet sizes and timing of streamed responses reveal the prompt topic. (4) Anderson, Amit, Goldsteen (IBM), "Is My Data in Your Retrieval Database? Membership Inference Attacks Against Retrieval Augmented Generation", arXiv 2405.20446. (5) Zeng et al., "The Good and The Bad: Exploring Privacy Issues in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)", Findings of ACL 2024, arXiv 2402.16893. (6) Qi et al., "Follow My Instruction and Spill the Beans: Scalable Data Extraction from Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems", ICLR 2025, arXiv 2402.17840. The first three exploit the network layer of a streaming model; the last three query the RAG system with crafted or injected prompts. Our setting differs: the observer is an ordinary customer, sends only legitimate messages, sees replies, decision fields and time, and we measure leakage with controlled statistics, then score defenses on security and utility. We also include a cross-user memory leakage study. We do not claim the table is complete.
